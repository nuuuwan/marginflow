# MarginFlow

What can we learn about how people changed their choices when we only know the
totals before and after? MarginFlow estimates how many people may have moved
from each choice to another, without needing records about individuals.

For example, I used results from polling divisions to explore how support may
have moved between candidates in Sri Lanka's 2019 and 2024 presidential
elections. This is useful because Sri Lanka has no nationwide exit polls. The
results are estimates, not records of how any individual voted.

I shared an earlier way of tackling this problem some time ago. MarginFlow
improves it by keeping all estimated percentages valid, checking the input for
mistakes, testing against examples where the answer is known, and producing
clear flow diagrams.

It could also help explore customers changing plans, people moving between job
or health categories, or changes in survey answers. All you need are comparable
group totals from two points in time.
